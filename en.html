Aquatic Animal Genomics and Molecular Breeding

Haiyang Liu, PhD　·　Pearl River Fisheries Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Fishery Sciences · Dept. of Aquatic Germplasm Resources and Genetic Breeding · Assistant Researcher

I received my PhD in Genetics from the Institute of Hydrobiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (University of Chinese Academy of Sciences) in 2017 and joined the Pearl River Fisheries Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Fishery Sciences in the same year, where I have worked since. My research covers the development and utilisation of aquatic animal germplasm resources, genetic dissection of economically important traits, genomic selection breeding, and the application of machine learning in aquaculture breeding. I have established an end-to-end technical system spanning genome assembly and annotation, population-genetic analysis, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and genomic selection. I have completed chromosome-level genome assemblies for more than ten aquatic species, including blotched snakehead, small snakehead, mud carp, darkbarbel catfish and Siniperca obscura; carried out a systematic survey of snakehead germplasm resources, characterising the genetic diversity, population structure and demographic history of the main cultured populations; dissected the genetic basis of growth and sex determination in snakeheads and other fishes, developing multiple sex-specific molecular markers; led the development of 50K liquid SNP breeding arrays for northern snakehead and blotched snakehead, built the associated genomic selection system, and explored machine-learning algorithms for accurate germplasm identification, complex-trait prediction and genomic estimated breeding values; and contributed to the breeding of the all-male hybrid snakehead variety “Xiongli No. 1” and its demonstration and extension in major production areas — forming a complete chain from genome analysis, germplasm assessment, trait dissection and array development through intelligent prediction and breeding application to commercialisation. To date I have published 31 SCI papers as first or corresponding author (79 SCI papers in total), with over 1,200 citations and an H-index of 20; I have led 12 research and commercialisation projects (total funding CNY 2.58 million) and participated in 6 national and provincial projects; I hold 12 granted patents (5 as first inventor, 2 of which have been commercially transferred); I contributed to the nationally approved hybrid snakehead variety “Xiongli No. 1” (GS-02-003-2022); I have received 7 science and technology awards, including the Fan Li Science and Technology Award (Second Prize, 2025); I participated in drafting 6 industry standards and 1 local standard; and I serve as a guest editor for Animals and Fishes and a reviewer for international journals including Nature Communications, Scientific Data and iScience.

Research

Genome resources and annotation

Chromosome-level and high-quality genome assemblies (PacBio HiFi / ONT / Hi-C) for snakeheads, mud carp, bagrid catfishes and sinipercine fishes, with gene-structure annotation from multi-species protein and RNA evidence, repeat and functional annotation. Completeness is tracked with BUSCO and compleasm; gaps are diagnosed and closed by targeted rescue.

Sex determination and trait genetics

High-density linkage mapping, QTL fine mapping, GWAS and transcriptome integration to dissect sex determination regions and growth-related loci; sex-specific molecular markers developed for snakeheads (C. argus, C. maculata, C. striata, C. asiatica), mud carp, darkbarbel catfish and bighead/silver carp, supporting all-male and monosex fry production.

Molecular breeding and SNP arrays

Led development of 50K liquid SNP breeding arrays for northern snakehead and blotched snakehead (patented and commercially licensed), including locus selection, probe design, genotyping quality control and cross-population validation; application to genomic selection, population assignment and parentage assignment.

Population genetics and germplasm assessment

Resequencing- and reduced-representation-based assessment of genetic diversity, population structure, linkage disequilibrium, selection signatures and demographic history in wild and selected populations, informing germplasm conservation and parental selection.

Machine learning and intelligent breeding

Applying machine learning across the breeding pipeline: accurate germplasm identification and population assignment from genomic and phenotypic data, prediction of complex traits, and genomic estimated breeding value (GEBV) assessment — supporting low-density-array genomic selection and parental selection.
